# World Through Time

An interactive world history map: borders from 4000 BC to today, a timeline built on Kyle's World History spreadsheet, 134 wars told as stories (territories, front lines, armies on the move led by their best-known generals, key events), how each denomination spread, and more than two thousand people: rulers beside the states they led and travellers, explorers and generals moving along their routes. The two hundred or so who mattered most wear a ring: tap one to read their life as a story, a page at a time, with famous pictures, what they won, made or discovered and what it changed, music that plays, anecdotes, and why they still matter. Click a state and its own row of stories opens: who ruled it or was there that year, then the rest of its people, nearest in time first. Even with no tab open, the map marks each state's main faith, puts swords on the borders of states at war and ships where they fight at sea, and keeps ships, caravans and trains moving along the trade routes of the time.

The map is drawn as an old atlas (a night chart in dark mode): painted relief, deserts, forests and ice under the states, seas shaded by depth with their names of each age and the great currents, and rivers and lakes that come and go with the years. Each state flies a small flag over its name, the flag of that year, and the map's colours are taken from the flags. About 120 cities stand on the map as the landmark they were known for at the time: Byzantium's walls, then Hagia Sophia, then Hagia Sophia with minarets.

The Migrations tab follows 68 peoples on the move, from the first farmers leaving Anatolia to the Syrian refugees: the Indo-Europeans and the Bantu, the Goths, Huns, Vandals, Franks and Lombards breaking into Rome, the Norse, Magyars and Turks, the Atlantic slave trade, the great Atlantic emigration, Partition and more. Each is a trail that grows across the map year by year with walkers, riders, wagons or ships moving along it; tap one to follow it step by step, with its key places and how many people moved. The biggest also show faintly on the ordinary map and in a clicked state's panel.

The Discovery button lists the great scientists, mathematicians, inventors, philosophers and artists among the ringed people, century by century, each a tap away from their story. The Leaders button beside it does the same for the rulers, conquerors, generals, statesmen, revolutionaries and reformers, filtered by role and by region.

Click a state and the timeline becomes the history of its country, told in its own periods: China's dynasties, Japan's eras, Rome from the Etruscans to the Italian Republic, Mali from the Ghana Empire to today, Mexico from the Olmec, Uzbekistan from the Oxus cities through the Samanids and Timur. 173 countries have one, each period with a short note, and the state picked on the map follows the country as you drag through them, to the empire that ruled it when it had no state of its own. A people the map draws across several of today's countries opens the country you clicked on, so the Taíno on Jamaica open Jamaica's timeline and the Celts in Ireland open Ireland's. A state that belongs to no country shows its own years instead. The back button or Escape returns to the world timeline, which, like every country's, ends this year.

Live at https://www.kyle-klugewicz.com/history-site/

It is a static site with no build step. Open `index.html` through any local web server, for example `python3 -m http.server`, and visit http://localhost:8000.

## Layout

- `index.html`: the whole app (D3 v7 from cdnjs).
- `data/`: what the page loads: `timeline.json`, `borders_<year>.json` (one per border year), `land.json`, `synopses.json`, `wars.json`, `religions.json`, `people.json` (everyone on the map and where they were each year), `commanders.json` (which army commanders get a figure at the head of their arrow), `ambient.json` (the trade routes whose traffic moves on the map), `migrations.json` (the peoples on the move), `stories/` (`index.json` names who has a story; each `<person id>.json` loads when its story opens) and `story_states.json` (which states each person with a story ruled, lived in or passed through, year by year, for a clicked state's row of stories). Portraits and story pictures load from Wikipedia at runtime, and story music from Wikimedia Commons; a face without a picture shows initials.
- `scripts/build_data.py`: rebuilds `timeline.json`, the border files and `land.json` from the historical-basemaps GeoJSON and the spreadsheet: `python3 scripts/build_data.py <geojson dir> <World_History.xlsx> data`.
- `content/wars/`: the war stories. `WAR_SCHEMA.md` describes the format, `gNN_gen.py` writes `wars_gNN.json`, `validate_wars.py` checks a file against the border data, and `python3 scripts/merge_wars.py` validates every batch and writes `data/wars.json`. Then `python3 scripts/war_extras.py` (needs shapely) adds what the ordinary map draws: where warring states' borders touch, and which armies and battles were at sea.
- `content/events/`: the stories of wars and great events, told in the same viewer as the people's stories. `_lib.py` has the page helpers (`scene`, `battle`, `turn`, `tale`, `work`, `made`, `figure`, `cost`, `song`, `legacy`) and the places the people's table lacks, and `eNN_*.py` hold the stories by era. A story given `war=` is that war's story in `data/wars.json` and belongs to every state the war touched; `states=` adds others. A clicked country's row takes a story when one of those states was the country's in the story's years, or when one of its places is on the country's land today. `python3 content/events/build_events.py` checks them (lengths that fit the card, places, years, state names against the border files) and writes `data/events/` (`index.json` lists every story with its years, states, place and the countries its places are in today, found on the latest border snapshot with `TODAY_FIX` for the places that coarse map gets wrong; each `<id>.json` loads when its story opens) and `media.txt`.
- `content/religions/`: the religion map. `denominations.json` lists the faiths, `hist_*.json` holds each denomination's history, `*_gen.py` writes one `zones_<region>.json` of dated anchor zones per region, `validate_zones.py` checks one, and `python3 content/religions/merge_religions.py content/religions/religions_old.json data/religions.json` merges them.
- `content/people/`: who is on the map. `_lib.py` holds the helpers and the table of places: `P(...)` adds a person, `R(...)` a reign that puts their face beside a state's name, and `G(...)` a place or a route they travel. `p01` to `p09` are people by era, `p10` to `p14` fill in each country's line of rulers, and `commanders.py` lists the generals worth a figure in the wars. `python3 content/people/build_people.py` checks every file against the border data and writes `data/people.json` and `data/commanders.json`.
- `content/stories/`: the stories. `chosen.py` lists the 203 people who get one (and so a ring), `_lib.py` has the page helpers (`life`, `win`, `made`, `work`, `song`, `tale`, `legacy`, each with an optional picture, year and place), and `sNN_*.py` hold the stories by era. `python3 content/stories/build_stories.py` checks them (lengths that fit the card, places, years, the legacy page last) and writes `data/stories/` and `media.txt`, the list of every Wikipedia picture and recording the stories ask for, to check from a machine that can reach Wikipedia. `python3 content/stories/build_states.py` (after `build_people.py`) works out which states each of them belongs to in each year, from their reigns and where the map puts them on the borders of that year, and writes `data/story_states.json`.
- `content/migrations/`: the peoples on the move. `_lib.py` explains the format: `M(...)` adds a migration (its people, kind, colour, years, numbers and an optional war or story), `S(...)` one of its steps and `mv(...)` a route of `[lon, lat, year]` points travelled on foot, by horse, wagon, ship, train or plane. `mNN_*.py` hold them by era; rank 1 shows on the ordinary map too. `python3 content/migrations/build_migrations.py` checks lengths, years, linked wars and stories, and that sea legs stay off land (needs shapely), and writes `data/migrations.json`.
- `content/ambient/build_ambient.py`: the trade routes and their traffic; writes `data/ambient.json` and checks that sea routes stay off land.
- `content/lineages/`: each country's own timeline. `_lib.py` explains the format: `L(...)` adds a country and `P(...)` one period of its history (a dynasty, a kingdom, a republic, a colonial era) with the names its states go by in the border files and a note. `lNN_*.py` hold the countries by region. `l00_shared.py` names the peoples and loose labels the map draws across several countries, which open the country under the click. `python3 content/lineages/build_lineages.py` checks every name and year against the border data, warns about snapshots where a country's heartland is held by a state it doesn't list, and writes `data/lineages.json`, which the page loads in the background; `--report` lists the biggest states that belong to no country yet (a click on one of those shows its own years on the map).
- `content/`: source text for the regional synopses.
- `scripts/build_basemap.py`: the painted land and sea in the map's projection, as tiles that sharpen as you zoom: `python3 scripts/build_basemap.py <source dir> data` writes `data/terrain/` (fetches Natural Earth and Blue Marble into the source dir; needs numpy, scipy and Pillow). It ends by running `scripts/build_small_world.py data`, which writes the half-size world pictures `land_s.webp` and `sea_s.jpg` that phones and the painted sea use; run that alone after changing `land0.webp` or `sea0.jpg`.
- `scripts/build_geo.py`: rivers, lakes and canals with the years they existed (`data/water.json`), and the names of oceans, seas, straits, mountains, deserts and peaks plus the ocean currents (`data/geo.json`): `python3 scripts/build_geo.py <source dir> data` (needs shapely and numpy).
- `content/flags/`: `flags.py` says which flag each state flies, year by year, with a note on each; `svg.py` draws the historical flags that flag-icons doesn't have. `python3 scripts/build_flags.py <source dir> data` writes `data/flags.json` (flags, timelines and the map tint of every state in every border file, taken from its flag so neighbours differ), the sprite `data/flags/sprite.webp` and one picture per flag for the side panel (needs node with Playwright, numpy, shapely and Pillow).
- `content/cities/`: `cities.py` lists the cities, each with its names over time, how important it was in each age and which landmark stood for it when; `icons.py` draws the landmarks with the small kit in `draw.py`. `python3 scripts/build_cities.py` checks them and writes `data/cities.json`.
- `content/stories/discovery.py`: the Discovery list of great minds. Each entry gives a person's fields (scientist, mathematician, inventor, philosopher, artist), what they were, what they are known for and the year of it, which sets their century. `python3 content/stories/build_discovery.py` checks it against the people and their stories and writes `data/discovery.json`; anyone listed before their story exists stays off the page until it does.
- `content/stories/leaders.py`: the Leaders list. Each entry gives a person's roles (ruler, conqueror, general, statesman, revolutionary, reformer), the regions they led in (Europe, the Middle East, Africa, Asia, the Americas), what they were, what they are known for and the year of it, which sets their century. `python3 content/stories/build_leaders.py` checks it the same way and writes `data/leaders.json`; `--report` lists the people with a story who are in neither Leaders nor Discovery.

## Keeping it light

The page should cost nothing while nobody is using it, since it is often left open on a phone or in a laptop tab.
- The sea, the ships, caravans and armies, the battle smoke and the pulsing rings on the map move while someone is using the page, and slow to a halt 20 seconds (12 on a phone) after the last pointer move, touch or key. Anything animated that never stops (a CSS animation with `infinite`) should pause under `.map-rest` like the others.
- The two canvases draw at no more than 1.5 pixels per CSS pixel. Only one level of terrain pictures stays in the map: coarser ones stand in only until the sharper ones have loaded.
- Only the five border snapshots nearest the shown year stay in memory.
- The Layers button lets a reader hide people, cities, names, flags, faith symbols, traffic or motion; the choice is kept in `localStorage` (`wtt-layers`). With motion off the map never animates. Ships, caravans and armies outside the view are not drawn.
